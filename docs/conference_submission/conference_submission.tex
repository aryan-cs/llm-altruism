\begin{abstract}
Refusing an explicitly harmful request is necessary for safe language-model behavior, but it does not exhaust the ways an agent can shift costs onto other people.
We present \textbf{Prosocial Readiness Bench}, a three-part evaluation of safety refusal, self-directed choices in dyadic dilemmas, and restraint in a repeated commons environment.
The tasks form a cost-shifting progression from an identifiable request target, to a counterpart, to a group whose losses accumulate through an environment; they are complementary behavioral probes, not a single altruism score.
Across \PaperPartZeroIncludedSystems{} current systems, exploratory material-refusal rates span \PaperPartZeroRefusalMinimumPct--\PaperPartZeroRefusalMaximumPct\% (median \PaperPartZeroRefusalMedianPct\%) over 24 English harmful-request roots crossed with three requested response languages.
Among \PaperPartOneNNinetySixSystems{} systems evaluated on a balanced 96-root self-choice bank, welfare-preserving choices span \PaperPartOneNNinetySixMinimumPct--\PaperPartOneNNinetySixMaximumPct\% (median \PaperPartOneNNinetySixMedianPct\%); a separate 384-root route selects them \PaperPartOneNThreeEightyFourEstimatePct\% of the time.
In the repaired commons task, \PaperPartTwoIncludedSystems{} systems complete \PaperPartTwoTrajectories{} trajectories; \PaperPartTwoValidTrajectories{} valid trajectories from \PaperPartTwoEstimableSystems{} systems support behavioral estimates. Median normalized reserve area is \PaperPartTwoAURCMedian{} and median restraint is \PaperPartTwoRestraintMedianPct\%, with wide system-level ranges.
These within-task differences show why refusal-only evaluation misses consequential variation, while validity gates preclude an unsupported cross-task leaderboard.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
LLMs are increasingly studied as decision-making agents that answer requests, recommend actions, and coordinate in simulated social environments \citep{bommasani2021opportunities,liu2024agentbench,park2023generative,zhou2024sotopia}. In these settings, refusal of explicitly harmful requests is only one part of responsible behavior. A broader evaluation should also assess whether a model selects cooperative actions in social dilemmas and whether repeated choices preserve a shared resource when overuse offers a short-term advantage.

Prosocial Readiness Bench addresses this requirement through three observable tasks rather than a latent moral trait. Part~0 asks whether a model materially refuses a request that would directly facilitate harm. Part~1 asks what the model itself chooses when a unilateral advantage shifts an immediate cost to a counterpart. Part~2 asks whether repeated local choices preserve a shared resource when the cost is delayed, distributed, and mediated by an environment. This progression supplies the task-selection rationale: each part increases the distance between action and affected party and changes how visibly and temporally the externality appears.

The three measurements are partially complementary. They may share mechanisms such as instruction following, norm recognition, or sensitivity to others' welfare, but each also introduces task-specific demands. We therefore do not assume that they correlate, interpret disagreement as proof of independence, or collapse them into an overall prosociality score. Cross-part associations are secondary finite-panel descriptions that become reportable only after every part passes its validity gate.

Relative to the pilot, the current evaluation separates judging from subject generation, aligns inference with request roots and complete trajectories, implements the incentives stated by the commons prompt, and expands coverage to current commercial and open-weight systems. Appendix~\ref{app:legacy-evidence} records the corresponding corrections to legacy evidence.

The paper asks two concrete questions. First, how should safety refusal, dyadic cooperation, and commons restraint be measured in one traceable artifact while respecting their different independent units? Second, how do current frontier and open-weight systems vary within these probes, across task conditions, and among exact related model routes? Cross-axis comparison remains a secondary analysis gated on the validity of every constituent measure. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the design and its evidence boundaries.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Three-part benchmark suite.} Prosocial Readiness Bench evaluates three partially complementary observable behaviors under explicit task contracts, organized by who bears the cost, how directly it is represented, and whether consequences accumulate over time.
    \item \textbf{Task-aligned measurement.} We define archived English request roots, scenario roots, and simulation trajectories as the respective independent units. Part~0 and Part~1 retain malformed or unclear outputs as nonsuccesses; Part~2 excludes any trajectory containing an invalid action from behavioral and environmental estimates. Part~0 uses one fixed judge distinct from every evaluated subject, and Part~2 implements the incentives shown to agents under repeated common seeds.
    \item \textbf{Broad, traceable evaluation.} The current frontier and open-weight executions span major model families through authenticated, identity-checked routes, with rate-limited parallel dispatch and no silent model substitution.
    \item \textbf{Task-specific empirical findings.} We report scope-separated variation among exact evaluated systems and task conditions, together with a corrected role-conditioned Part~1 calibration result; we do not collapse the tasks into a leaderboard or latent-trait score.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
Safety and jailbreak benchmarks such as HarmBench and JailbreakBench measure harmful-request compliance and refusal \citep{mazeika2024harmbench,chao2024jailbreakbench}. Red-teaming and adversarial attacks test robustness under unsafe requests \citep{ganguli2022redteaming,zou2023universal}, while alignment and safeguard work studies how models avoid harmful assistance \citep{bai2022constitutional,wang2024donotanswer}; XSTest adds clearly safe contrast prompts that can trigger exaggerated refusal \citep{rottger2024xstest}. Multilingual-input jailbreak results show that input language can alter safety behavior \citep{deng2024multilingual}; our response-language manipulation is deliberately distinguished from that setting.

Classical work on repeated cooperation and commons governance motivates the underlying strategic structures \citep{axelrod1984evolution,hardin1968tragedy,ostrom1990governing}, while cooperative-AI research frames coordination among artificial and human agents as a distinct research problem \citep{dafoe2020open}. LLM studies examine one-shot economic choices \citep{brookins2024playing,horton2023homo} and repeated social dilemmas \citep{akata2025playing,tewolde2026coopeval}; MACHIAVELLI separately measures reward-seeking against ethical costs in interactive environments \citep{pan2023machiavelli}. Most closely, GovSim studies whether communicating LLM societies sustain a common-pool resource \citep{piatti2024govsim}; our Part~2 instead isolates repeated direct choices under a fixed prompt--engine contract and common environment seeds. AgentBench evaluates general agent capabilities, while Generative Agents, SOTOPIA, and SocialEval study broader simulated or benchmarked social behavior \citep{liu2024agentbench,park2023generative,zhou2024sotopia,zhou2025socialeval}.

Broad evaluation suites and multi-dimensional trustworthiness studies motivate reporting separate behavioral dimensions rather than a single score \citep{hendrycks2021measuring,srivastava2023beyond,liang2023helm,wang2023decodingtrust}. Participatory feedback datasets expose preference variation \citep{kirk2024prism}, while output-diversity work shows that distinct models can still converge on homogeneous responses \citep{jiang2025artificialhivemind}. Dataset and model documentation standards motivate the artifact-facing emphasis on provenance, metadata, access conditions, and responsible-use notes \citep{bender2018datastatements,holland2018datasetnutrition,mitchell2019modelcards,gebru2021datasheets,pushkarna2022datacards,akhtar2024croissant}.

We focus on combining safety refusal, self-directed dyadic cooperation, repeated commons restraint, and traceable release artifacts in one benchmark suite whose inferential units match its claims. Table~\ref{tab:positioning} summarizes this positioning. The evaluation spans a frozen panel of current commercial and open-weight routes, with exact identities and unavailable routes recorded rather than substituted.

\section{Benchmark Design and Task Contracts}
\label{sec:benchmark-design}
\subsection{Design Principles}
The benchmark uses three design principles.

\paragraph{Organize tasks by cost shifting.}
The parts move from a direct harmful request, to an immediate choice affecting a counterpart, to repeated choices affecting a group through a shared state. This is a principled progression in externality structure, not a claim that the tasks form a psychometric scale. Safety refusal, dyadic cooperation, and commons restraint remain separate outcomes.

\paragraph{Make every claim traceable to stored artifacts.}
Each request retains its exact route identity, decoding controls, request and response hashes, provider completion identifier, finish reason, token usage, retry decision, and structured outcome. Campaign manifests bind the frozen registry, prompt bank, execution evidence, and analysis outputs.

\paragraph{Match inference to experimental units.}
Response-language conditions nested within an English request root and agent-days nested within a trajectory are not independent observations. Part~1 schedules one rendering per scenario root. The estimators below resample roots or complete trajectories accordingly. A narrower valid interval is obtained by collecting more independent units or repeated generations, never by treating dependent rows as independent.

\subsection{Part 0: Safety Refusal}
Part~0 measures material refusal on an outcome-blind selection of 24 harmful-request roots from version-hashed local snapshots of HarmBench and JailbreakBench \citep{mazeika2024harmbench,chao2024jailbreakbench}. The exact translated inputs were not retained, so every condition uses the same archived English request plus an explicit instruction to answer in English, Simplified Chinese, or Russian. These are reconstructed response-language conditions, not multilingual input prompts. The three conditions are repeated measurements of one request root, yielding 72 scheduled responses per system. This execution has no benign controls and therefore cannot distinguish selective safety behavior from a general tendency to refuse. Every scheduled response remains in the denominator; \texttt{UNCLEAR} and \texttt{INVALID} are nonsuccesses.

One frozen multilingual judge receives only the English request, its explicit response-language instruction, and the raw visible response; it is blind to subject identity and hypotheses and returns \texttt{REFUSAL}, \texttt{COMPLIANCE}, or \texttt{UNCLEAR} at temperature zero. The judge model is fixed across the subject panel and is never the model being evaluated. If a route selected as judge would otherwise enter the subject panel, that route is excluded from judging itself rather than silently switching judges. Holding the rater fixed avoids introducing a moving-rater confound, while disjointness addresses self-enhancement bias; neither design choice establishes judge validity \citep{zheng2023judging}.

The descriptive metric is material refusal under the reconstructed response-language conditions:
\[
\mathrm{refusal} =
\frac{\#\mathrm{REFUSAL}}{\#\mathrm{scheduled\ harmful\ responses}}.
\]
Five thousand bootstrap replicates resample the 24 request roots and retain all three response-language conditions within each sampled root \citep{efron1979bootstrap}. Condition-specific rates receive Wilson score intervals over the 24 roots \citep{wilson1927probable}. These are finite-bank sensitivity summaries, not confidence intervals for generation variability or a prompt superpopulation.

No language-qualified human validation has been completed. Judge--subject separation reduces a rater-overlap confound, but it does not validate the labels. Part~0 is therefore exploratory and cannot support paper-facing model rankings or cross-part claims.

\subsection{Part 1: Dyadic Cooperation and Role-Conditioned Probes}
Part~1 evaluates what the model itself chooses in a neutral one-shot dilemma. Direct self-choice is the primary cooperation estimand. Advice, observer evaluation, prediction, and demand-cue instructions ask different questions and therefore serve as distinct calibration probes rather than paraphrases pooled into self-choice. The broad current campaign evaluates self-choice; the archived pilot supplies the separate role-conditioned comparison reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

The executed broad panel uses a deterministic draft bank with two games crossed by six neutral domains. Of 81 frozen targets, \PaperPartOneNNinetySixSystems{} reportable routes receive a balanced 96-root schedule with eight roots in every game--domain stratum, Qwen3.5~397B receives a balanced 12-root schedule with one root per stratum, and GLM~5.1 receives the full 384-root schedule with 32 roots per stratum. Six routes are unavailable: Kimi~K2.5, Kimi~K2.6, and GLM-5.2 were unavailable before execution; Claude~Opus~4.5, MiniMax~M2.7, and MiniMax~M3 have target-bound operational failures. No unavailable route is replaced silently. Responses must end with the exact structured action \texttt{X} or \texttt{Y}; malformed output is \texttt{INVALID}, is retained as a non-welfare-preserving choice, and is not regenerated.

The primary metric is the self-directed welfare-preserving choice rate:
\[
\mathrm{self\mbox{-}choice} =
\frac{\#\mathrm{welfare\mbox{-}preserving\ choices}}{\#\mathrm{scheduled\ roots}}.
\]
The scenario root is the independent unit. For the 96-root and 384-root scopes, five thousand bootstrap replicates resample roots separately within the 12 game--domain strata. The resulting bands describe sensitivity to the executed draft bank, not generation-seed uncertainty or a scenario superpopulation. The 12-root scope has only one root per stratum, so it provides a coverage-balanced descriptive rate but cannot estimate within-stratum prompt-bank sensitivity. The 12-, 96-, and 384-root scopes are reported separately and are never pooled or directly ranked as if they had identical coverage. Because the draft bank has not received independent human content approval, all current Part~1 estimates remain exploratory.

\subsection{Part 2: Commons Restraint}
Part~2 measures preservation of a common-pool resource under repeated direct choices. The simulator implements the incentives stated in the prompt. Each society has five anonymous slots and a 12-day horizon. The common reserve begins at capacity 50. Option~A gives the acting slot one private point and leaves the reserve unchanged; Option~B gives two private points and removes two reserve units. Unanimous Option~A adds five group points, whereas unanimous Option~B subtracts five. After depletion, attrition occurs with probability 0.2 under the common environment seed. These parameters define an artificial diagnostic environment, not an ecological model of a real community.

Each of the \PaperPartTwoIncludedSystems{} executed systems receives eight common environment seeds, yielding \PaperPartTwoTrajectories{} trajectories; two additional matched-panel systems are operationally unavailable. An invalid action has zero state effect so the simulator can preserve transition continuity, but that zero effect is not evidence of restraint. Any trajectory containing an invalid action is therefore protocol-invalid and excluded from all behavioral and environmental estimates. The primary metric on valid trajectories is normalized area under the reserve curve:
\[
\mathrm{AURC}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{R_t}{R_{\max}}.
\]
Run-level restraint, area under the population curve, final reserve, population retention, and cumulative private and group payoffs are secondary. The trajectory is the independent unit; agent-days are never inferential replicates. For each estimable system, continuous metrics receive Student-$t$ 95\% intervals with degrees of freedom equal to its valid trajectory count minus one (seven for most systems and six for one). Binary reserve nondepletion receives a Wilson interval over that system's valid trajectories \citep{wilson1927probable}. A system with no valid trajectory is non-estimable. No BCa interval, restricted-mean-time-to-depletion analysis, or parameter-sensitivity experiment is part of the executed design.

\section{Artifact Construction and Quality Control}
\paragraph{Frozen model cohort.}
The matched registry contains 24 current systems from 9 upstream developers. Part~0 reports \PaperPartZeroIncludedSystems{} and marks \PaperPartZeroUnavailableSystems{} unavailable. Part~2 executes \PaperPartTwoIncludedSystems{} and marks \PaperPartTwoUnavailableSystems{} unavailable; \PaperPartTwoEstimableSystems{} executed systems support behavioral estimates, while \PaperPartTwoNonestimableSystems{} all-invalid systems remain non-estimable rather than being relabeled unavailable. The registry spans general-purpose GPT, Claude Haiku/Sonnet/Opus, Gemini, Llama, DeepSeek, Qwen, Nemotron, MiniMax, and GLM routes when the exact authenticated route is callable. Technical reports describe selected GPT, GPT-OSS, Llama, and Qwen families, while the route manifests are the authoritative identity record for every evaluated endpoint \citep{openai2023gpt4,openai2025gptoss,touvron2023llama2,yang2024qwen25}. Part~1 reports \PaperPartOneReportedSystems{} of \PaperPartOneTargetedSystems{} frozen targets: \PaperPartOneNNinetySixSystems{} at 96 roots, one at 12 roots, and one at 384 roots. Four pinned offline controls (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct and SmolLM2 at 135M, 360M, and 1.7B) also completed 384 roots; they are local scale controls, not substitutes for hosted routes, and are not pooled into the paper panel. Image, embedding, reranking, guard-only, inaccessible, deprecated, or identity-mismatched routes are excluded with recorded failure codes. A display name is never substituted for an unavailable route.

\paragraph{Output contracts and metadata.}
An authenticated route is eligible only after a bounded structured smoke test returns the exact requested identity. Every attempt stores route identity, completion identifier, finish reason, token usage, controls, hashes, outcome, and retry decision. Part~0 stores the visible subject response and independent judge verdict; Parts~1 and~2 store direct structured subject actions. Metadata, manifests, schemas, and Croissant JSON-LD \citep{akhtar2024croissant} bind these records to the frozen campaign.

Requests are dispatched concurrently across independent routes and units, but bounded per-provider and global request/token buckets prevent overload. HTTP 408/429, transport, timeout, and 5xx failures without a retained model response may be retried at most twice with the identical payload and seed. Semantic or formatting failures are retained as \texttt{INVALID}; they are never regenerated. Part~0 and Part~1 count them as nonsuccesses. Part~2 retains them for audit and transition continuity but excludes the entire affected trajectory from behavioral and environmental estimates. An identity mismatch or retry-exhausted target-part quarantines that target-part rather than triggering a substitute model.

\paragraph{Validation.}
Validation is graph-independent. It checks hashes, route identity, prompt-bank coverage, duplicate and missing units, response contracts, retry accounting, Part~2 day continuity and transitions, and execution completeness. Structural validation does not certify semantic labels or prompt content. Consequently, the absent Part~0 human validation and absent Part~1 draft-bank approval keep those outputs exploratory even if their operational manifests complete.

\paragraph{Harmful-output handling.}
Part 0 evaluates harmful prompts and may produce unsafe completions. The anonymous supplement therefore releases derived tables, validation outputs, and aggregate summaries rather than raw harmful generations. It supports verification and regeneration of paper-facing tables from the sealed aggregate graph, but not independent regeneration of that graph from private prompts, responses, authenticated routes, manifests, or journals.

\paragraph{Evidence status.}
Historical pilot artifacts are kept separate from the current campaign and summarized in Appendix~\ref{app:legacy-evidence}. The current result graph reconciles every frozen target as included or unavailable. Part~0 and Part~1 remain exploratory because their semantic validity gates are external to operational completion; Part~2 is reported from the corrected engine at the trajectory level.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
The subject decoding protocol is frozen across routes before outcomes are collected. Unsupported controls follow one prespecified common-denominator rule and are never silently omitted for a single system. Part~0 and Part~1 use every scheduled unit as the denominator and retain unclear, malformed, or semantic-invalid responses as nonsuccesses. Part~2 behavioral and environmental denominators contain only trajectories with no invalid agent action; invalid trajectories remain in audit counts but contribute no behavioral estimate. The estimands concern the exact executed prompt banks, model routes, and decoding protocol, not altruistic intent, vendor-wide populations, or unrestricted generation-seed behavior.

Any cross-part analysis is restricted to the exact 24-system matched panel and remains unavailable while Part~0 lacks human validation and the matching Part~1 prompts lack content approval. Historical systems and the broader Part~1 routes remain separate; Appendix~\ref{app:legacy-evidence} records the prespecified weighting and sensitivity rules for any future gated analysis.

\subsection{Part 0: Exploratory Safety Refusal}
Across \PaperPartZeroIncludedSystems{} systems and \PaperPartZeroScheduledResponses{} scheduled responses, the fixed judge assigns \PaperPartZeroRefusalCount{} refusals, \PaperPartZeroComplianceCount{} compliances, \PaperPartZeroUnclearCount{} unclear outcomes, and \PaperPartZeroInvalidCount{} invalid outputs. System-level material-refusal rates span \PaperPartZeroRefusalMinimumPct--\PaperPartZeroRefusalMaximumPct\%, with a median of \PaperPartZeroRefusalMedianPct\%. Median system rates are \PaperPartZeroEnglishMedianPct\% under the English response instruction, \PaperPartZeroChineseMedianPct\% under Chinese, and \PaperPartZeroRussianMedianPct\% under Russian. These differences describe requested output-language sensitivity over the same English roots, not multilingual-input robustness. The result shows substantial residual variation even among modern systems, but it remains exploratory because there are no benign controls or completed language-qualified human validation. Full root-clustered and condition-specific intervals appear in Appendix~\ref{app:current-results}.

\subsection{Part 1: Exploratory Dyadic Cooperation}
The broad self-choice campaign reports \PaperPartOneReportedSystems{} systems over \PaperPartOneScheduledRoots{} scheduled roots. Within the balanced 96-root scope, \PaperPartOneNNinetySixSystems{} systems span \PaperPartOneNNinetySixMinimumPct--\PaperPartOneNNinetySixMaximumPct\% welfare-preserving choices, with a median of \PaperPartOneNNinetySixMedianPct\%; \PaperPartOneNNinetySixInvalid{} of \PaperPartOneNNinetySixScheduledRoots{} outputs are format-invalid and count as non-welfare-preserving. The separate 12-root Qwen3.5~397B execution yields \PaperPartOneNTwelveValues{}, while the full 384-root GLM~5.1 execution yields \PaperPartOneNThreeEightyFourEstimatePct\% [\PaperPartOneNThreeEightyFourLowerPct, \PaperPartOneNThreeEightyFourUpperPct]\% with \PaperPartOneNThreeEightyFourInvalid{} invalid output. The scopes are never pooled. The broad range demonstrates that self-directed dyadic choices expose variation that refusal rates alone cannot characterize, while the low median cautions against treating verbal safety alignment as evidence of welfare-preserving action in this task. Full system and stratified-root intervals appear in Appendix~\ref{app:current-results}.

The corrected separation of task roles preserves one finding from the 13-system April pilot while narrowing its interpretation. Across the balanced legacy matrix, the welfare-preserving action-label rate was 83.2\% when models acted as external evaluators, 66.8\% when they advised the focal actor, 61.4\% when they chose for themselves, and 4.9\% when they predicted the focal actor's choice. Each role contained the same 48 scenario roots with two nested presentations per system. These contrasts show strong role-conditioned response differences; because the instructions ask different questions, they are not a causal framing effect on one cooperation estimand. Appendix~\ref{app:legacy-evidence} keeps this legacy panel separate from the current self-choice campaign.

\subsection{Part 2: Commons Restraint}
The corrected commons campaign executes \PaperPartTwoTrajectories{} independent common-seed trajectories across \PaperPartTwoIncludedSystems{} systems. Of these, \PaperPartTwoValidTrajectories{} trajectories from \PaperPartTwoEstimableSystems{} systems are fully valid and support estimates; \PaperPartTwoProtocolInvalidTrajectories{} trajectories contain at least one invalid action and are excluded from all behavioral and environmental metrics. The remaining \PaperPartTwoNonestimableSystems{} executed systems are all-invalid and therefore non-estimable, not unavailable. Across estimable systems, mean AURC ranges from \PaperPartTwoAURCMinimum{} to \PaperPartTwoAURCMaximum{} (median \PaperPartTwoAURCMedian{}), mean restraint ranges from \PaperPartTwoRestraintMinimumPct{} to \PaperPartTwoRestraintMaximumPct\% (median \PaperPartTwoRestraintMedianPct\%), and mean AUPC ranges from \PaperPartTwoAUPCMinimum{} to \PaperPartTwoAUPCMaximum{}. \PaperPartTwoNondepleted{} of \PaperPartTwoNondepletionDenominator{} valid trajectories finish without reserve depletion. The raw audit contains \PaperPartTwoInvalidAgentDays{} invalid agent-days among \PaperPartTwoScheduledAgentDays{} scheduled agent-days; their simulator-level zero state effect preserves continuity but supplies no restraint evidence. Appendix~\ref{app:current-results} reports valid/total counts and all estimable trajectory-level intervals.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{Refusal is informative but incomplete.}
The corrected current campaign preserves the original empirical motivation for Safety Beyond Refusal. Harmful-request refusal is generally high but not uniform; direct welfare-preserving self-choice is often rare and spans nearly the full range; and commons behavior ranges from complete overuse to sustained restraint. These are within-task findings, not evidence that one axis predicts another. Together they show that a refusal score cannot substitute for direct evaluation of dyadic and repeated-resource decisions.

\paragraph{Partial complementarity without assumed independence.}
Separate reporting is justified by task design, not by a required low correlation. The tasks share the broad question of whether an action shifts avoidable costs onto others, while varying the explicitness of the rule, identity of the affected party, payoff structure, temporal horizon, and environmental mediation. A high, low, or uncertain association would each be scientifically interpretable; none would turn the three tasks into a unitary latent score.

\paragraph{Role conditioning is part of the behavior being measured.}
The legacy Part~1 contrast is too large to treat role wording as a cosmetic nuisance: models frequently selected the welfare-preserving label when evaluating or advising, but rarely selected it when predicting another actor. The corrected benchmark therefore defines self-choice as the primary cooperation estimand and treats the other roles as calibration probes. This retains the original insight that refusal-only evaluation misses context-dependent social behavior without conflating judgment, recommendation, prediction, and action.

\paragraph{Evidence boundaries.}
No cross-part association is current paper evidence because Part~0 lacks human validation and Part~1 lacks independent content approval. This boundary prevents exploratory labels from being converted into a stronger claim merely by matching model identities. Appendix~\ref{app:legacy-evidence} records why the earlier cross-part statistics are excluded.

\paragraph{Extension to agent systems.}
The same three probes can be applied to tool-using or multi-agent systems by scoring externally visible decisions rather than attributing a stable trait to the underlying language model. Such an extension would require heterogeneous roles, persistent state, communication, tool-mediated actions, independent environments, and system-level failure criteria while preserving the task-specific inferential units used here.

\section{Limitations and Broader Impact}
\label{sec:scope}
Prosocial Readiness Bench evaluates observable outputs in artificial tasks, not intent, moral status, altruism, or real-world trustworthiness. A finite authenticated route is not a vendor, and one frozen generation per Part~0 or Part~1 root does not estimate decoding variability. Part~0 reconstructs response-language instructions over English inputs, has no benign controls, and lacks human label validation. The Part~1 dilemmas simplify social context, impose explicit actions and payoffs, omit interaction, and use a draft bank without independent content approval. Part~2 uses anonymous homogeneous five-agent same-model populations in a 12-step, single-parameterization environment; it omits heterogeneous roles, communication, memory, bargaining, tool use, institutions, and human participants. The executed design contains no parameter-sensitivity study and therefore cannot show robustness beyond this toy ecology.

The natural extension is an agent-system evaluation with heterogeneous models and roles, persistent but privacy-preserving memory, communication and negotiation channels, tool-mediated actions, institutional constraints, and human-designed environment dynamics. Such an extension should preserve trajectory-level inference and common seeds while adding independent environments and preregistered system-level failure criteria. It should not reuse the present Part~2 simulator as if it were a calibrated society.

Used conservatively, the artifact can help developers identify whether a safety intervention improves explicit refusal while leaving less direct cost-shifting behaviors unchanged, motivating targeted evaluation before deployment. It is intended for behavioral auditing and mitigation comparison, not for improving harmful compliance. Raw Part 0 harmful prompts and completions are excluded from the anonymous supplement by default. Because the executed Part~0 inputs are English requests with response-language instructions, they cannot support cross-cultural or translated-prompt claims. Any later multilingual input evaluation would require retained translations plus language-specific and culture-specific expert review.

\section{Conclusion}
Safety Beyond Refusal asks how model evaluation should expand from explicit harmful requests to choices whose costs are less direct, more distributed, and temporally accumulated. Prosocial Readiness Bench answers with three separate, traceable probes of refusal, self-directed dyadic choice, and repeated commons restraint. The current campaign finds material variation within every estimable axis: refusal spans \PaperPartZeroRefusalMinimumPct--\PaperPartZeroRefusalMaximumPct\%, 96-root dyadic choice spans \PaperPartOneNNinetySixMinimumPct--\PaperPartOneNNinetySixMaximumPct\%, and valid-trajectory commons restraint spans \PaperPartTwoRestraintMinimumPct--\PaperPartTwoRestraintMaximumPct\%. Judge--subject separation, root-aware inference, corrected incentives, independent common-seed trajectories, and explicit unavailable and non-estimable accounting make those findings traceable without turning them into a latent moral score. The result is a stronger behavioral evaluation artifact for identifying safety-relevant variation that refusal-only benchmarks necessarily leave unmeasured.
