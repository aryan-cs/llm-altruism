\begin{abstract}
Refusing an explicitly harmful request is necessary for safe language-model behavior, but it does not exhaust the ways an agent can shift costs onto other people.
We present \textbf{Prosocial Readiness Bench}, a three-part evaluation spanning safety refusal under reconstructed response-language instructions, dyadic cooperation through self-directed one-shot choices, and commons restraint in a repeated resource environment.
The tasks form a cost-shifting progression: the potential cost moves from an identifiable request target, to a dyadic counterpart, to a group whose losses accumulate through an environment.
They are partially complementary stress tests, not interchangeable measures of altruism and not a single readiness score.
An audit of the original pilot found that its Part~0 labels and Part~2 simulator do not support model-level conclusions, so we withdraw those legacy estimates and all cross-part correlations.
We retain a clearly labeled descriptive legacy Part~1 result and freeze current-model executions at 24 archived harmful-request roots crossed with three response-language instructions, balanced Part~1 schedules of 96 roots for 75 routes and 12 roots for two extreme-latency routes, a full 384-root Part~1 schedule for GLM~5.1, and eight common-seed Part~2 trajectories for 24 systems.
Part~0 lacks human validation and Part~1 uses an unapproved draft bank, so both executions remain exploratory and current-model outcomes are withheld pending completion and validity checks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
LLMs are increasingly studied as decision-making agents that answer requests, recommend actions, and coordinate in simulated social environments \citep{bommasani2021opportunities,liu2024agentbench,park2023generative,zhou2024sotopia}. In these settings, refusal of explicitly harmful requests is only one part of responsible behavior. A broader evaluation should also assess whether a model selects cooperative actions in social dilemmas and whether repeated choices preserve a shared resource when overuse offers a short-term advantage.

Prosocial Readiness Bench addresses this requirement through three observable tasks rather than a latent moral trait. Part~0 asks whether a model materially refuses a request that would directly facilitate harm. Part~1 asks what the model itself chooses when a unilateral advantage shifts an immediate cost to a counterpart. Part~2 asks whether repeated local choices preserve a shared resource when the cost is delayed, distributed, and mediated by an environment. This progression supplies the task-selection rationale: each part increases the distance between action and affected party and changes how visibly and temporally the externality appears.

The three measurements are partially complementary. They may share mechanisms such as instruction following, norm recognition, or sensitivity to others' welfare, but each also introduces task-specific demands. We therefore do not assume that they correlate, interpret disagreement as proof of independence, or collapse them into an overall prosociality score. Cross-part associations are secondary finite-panel descriptions that become reportable only after every part passes its validity gate.

The original April pilot covered 13 related local/open-weight variants. An end-to-end audit showed that Part~0 used rationale-contaminated automated labels without quantitative human validation and that Part~2 described incentives the engine never implemented while providing only one trajectory per model. Those defects invalidate the corresponding model rates and every correlation using them. We remove those results rather than cosmetically changing their intervals. The surviving Part~1 self-choice summary is retained only as legacy descriptive context; the frozen current-model executions are outcome-independent and are not pooled with it.

The paper asks two concrete questions. First, how should safety refusal, dyadic cooperation, and commons restraint be measured in one traceable artifact while respecting their different independent units? Second, how do current frontier and open-weight systems vary across these probes, and which cross-axis comparisons survive the required validity gates? Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the design and its evidence boundaries.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cost-shifting task progression.} We motivate three partially complementary behavioral probes by who bears the cost, how directly it is represented, and whether consequences accumulate over time.
    \item \textbf{Corrected measurement design.} We define archived English request roots, scenario roots, and complete simulation trajectories as the respective independent units; malformed and unclear outputs remain in denominators.
    \item \textbf{Separated judging and repaired simulation.} Part~0 uses one fixed judge distinct from every evaluated subject, while its absent human validation is stated explicitly. Part~2 implements the incentives shown to agents and uses repeated common-seed trajectories.
    \item \textbf{Broad, traceable evaluation.} The current frontier and open-weight executions span major model families through authenticated, identity-checked routes, with rate-limited parallel dispatch and no silent model substitution.
    \item \textbf{Transparent evidence boundary.} We withdraw invalid legacy statistics, retain only defensible legacy Part~1 context, and label the executed current Part~0 and Part~1 panels exploratory rather than promoting them as confirmatory evidence.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
Safety and jailbreak benchmarks such as HarmBench and JailbreakBench measure harmful-request compliance and refusal \citep{mazeika2024harmbench,chao2024jailbreakbench}. Red-teaming, adversarial-attack, and safeguard work tests robustness under unsafe requests \citep{ganguli2022redteaming,bai2022constitutional,zou2023universal,wang2024donotanswer}; XSTest adds clearly safe contrast prompts that can trigger exaggerated refusal \citep{rottger2024xstest}. Multilingual jailbreak evaluations further motivate treating language conditions as an explicit experimental factor \citep{deng2024multilingual}.

Classical work on repeated cooperation and commons governance motivates the underlying strategic structures \citep{axelrod1984evolution,hardin1968tragedy,ostrom1990governing}, while cooperative-AI research frames coordination among artificial and human agents as a distinct research problem \citep{dafoe2020open}. LLM studies evaluate one-shot and repeated strategic choices \citep{akata2025playing,brookins2024playing,horton2023homo,tewolde2026coopeval}. Most closely, GovSim studies whether communicating LLM societies sustain a common-pool resource \citep{piatti2024govsim}; our Part~2 instead isolates repeated direct choices under a fixed prompt--engine contract and common environment seeds. AgentBench evaluates general agent capabilities, while Generative Agents, SOTOPIA, and SocialEval study broader simulated or benchmarked social behavior \citep{liu2024agentbench,park2023generative,zhou2024sotopia,zhou2025socialeval}.

Broad evaluation suites and multi-dimensional trustworthiness studies motivate reporting separate behavioral dimensions rather than a single score \citep{hendrycks2021measuring,srivastava2023beyond,liang2023helm,wang2023decodingtrust}. Participatory feedback datasets expose preference variation \citep{kirk2024prism}, while output-diversity work shows that distinct models can still converge on homogeneous responses \citep{jiang2025artificialhivemind}. Dataset and model documentation standards motivate the artifact-facing emphasis on provenance, metadata, access conditions, and responsible-use notes \citep{bender2018datastatements,holland2018datasetnutrition,mitchell2019modelcards,gebru2021datasheets,pushkarna2022datacards,akhtar2024croissant}.

We focus on combining safety refusal, self-directed dyadic cooperation, repeated commons restraint, and traceable release artifacts in one benchmark suite whose inferential units match its claims. Table~\ref{tab:positioning} summarizes this positioning. Unlike the original local-model pilot, the current execution targets frozen commercial and open-weight routes and treats historical routes as a separate descriptive cohort.

\section{Benchmark Design and Task Contracts}
\label{sec:benchmark-design}
\subsection{Design Principles}
The benchmark uses three design principles.

\paragraph{Organize tasks by cost shifting.}
The parts move from a direct harmful request, to an immediate choice affecting a counterpart, to repeated choices affecting a group through a shared state. This is a principled progression in externality structure, not a claim that the tasks form a psychometric scale. Safety refusal, dyadic cooperation, and commons restraint remain separate outcomes.

\paragraph{Make every claim traceable to stored artifacts.}
Each request retains its exact route identity, decoding controls, request and response hashes, provider completion identifier, finish reason, token usage, retry decision, and structured outcome. Campaign manifests bind the frozen registry, prompt bank, execution evidence, and analysis outputs.

\paragraph{Match inference to experimental units.}
Response-language conditions nested within an English request root and agent-days nested within a trajectory are not independent observations. Part~1 schedules one rendering per scenario root. The estimators below resample roots or complete trajectories accordingly. A narrower valid interval is obtained by collecting more independent units or repeated generations, never by treating dependent rows as independent.

\subsection{Part 0: Safety Refusal}
Part~0 measures material refusal on an outcome-blind selection of 24 harmful-request roots from version-hashed local snapshots of HarmBench and JailbreakBench \citep{mazeika2024harmbench,chao2024jailbreakbench}. The exact translated inputs were not retained, so every condition uses the same archived English request plus an explicit instruction to answer in English, Simplified Chinese, or Russian. These are reconstructed response-language conditions, not multilingual input prompts. The three conditions are repeated measurements of one request root, yielding 72 scheduled responses per system. This execution has no benign controls and therefore cannot distinguish selective safety behavior from a general tendency to refuse. Every scheduled response remains in the denominator; \texttt{UNCLEAR} and \texttt{INVALID} are nonsuccesses.

One frozen multilingual judge receives only the English request, its explicit response-language instruction, and the raw visible response; it is blind to subject identity and hypotheses and returns \texttt{REFUSAL}, \texttt{COMPLIANCE}, or \texttt{UNCLEAR} at temperature zero. The judge model is fixed across the subject panel and is never the model being evaluated. If a route selected as judge would otherwise enter the subject panel, that route is excluded from judging itself rather than silently switching judges. Holding the rater fixed avoids introducing a moving-rater confound, while disjointness addresses self-enhancement bias; neither design choice establishes judge validity \citep{zheng2023judging}.

The descriptive metric is material refusal under the reconstructed response-language conditions:
\[
\mathrm{refusal} =
\frac{\#\mathrm{REFUSAL}}{\#\mathrm{scheduled\ harmful\ responses}}.
\]
Five thousand bootstrap replicates resample the 24 request roots and retain all three response-language conditions within each sampled root \citep{efron1979bootstrap}. Condition-specific rates receive Wilson score intervals over the 24 roots \citep{wilson1927probable}. These are finite-bank sensitivity summaries, not confidence intervals for generation variability or a prompt superpopulation.

No language-qualified human validation has been completed. Judge--subject separation reduces a rater-overlap confound, but it does not validate the labels. Part~0 is therefore exploratory and cannot support paper-facing model rankings or cross-part claims.

\subsection{Part 1: Dyadic Cooperation}
Part~1 evaluates what the model itself chooses in a neutral one-shot dilemma. Advice, observer evaluation, prediction, and demand-cue prompts are omitted from the self-choice construct because they ask different questions; they are not paraphrases of self-directed choice. This distinction resolves the ambiguity in the original pilot's pooled ``frame'' rate.

The executed broad panel uses a deterministic draft bank with two games crossed by six neutral domains. Seventy-five observed routes receive a balanced 96-root schedule with eight roots in every game--domain stratum. Two extreme-latency routes, MiniMax~M3 and Qwen3.5~397B, receive a balanced 12-root schedule with one root per stratum. GLM~5.1 receives the full 384-root schedule with 32 roots per stratum. The other three targets, Kimi~K2.5, Kimi~K2.6, and GLM-5.2, were unavailable and are reported as such rather than replaced. In total, 78 of 81 targeted routes were observed. Responses must end with the exact structured action \texttt{X} or \texttt{Y}; malformed output is \texttt{INVALID}, is retained as a non-welfare-preserving choice, and is not regenerated.

The primary metric is the self-directed welfare-preserving choice rate:
\[
\mathrm{self\mbox{-}choice} =
\frac{\#\mathrm{welfare\mbox{-}preserving\ choices}}{\#\mathrm{scheduled\ roots}}.
\]
The scenario root is the independent unit. For the 96-root and 384-root scopes, five thousand bootstrap replicates resample roots separately within the 12 game--domain strata. The resulting bands describe sensitivity to the executed draft bank, not generation-seed uncertainty or a scenario superpopulation. The 12-root scope has only one root per stratum, so it provides a coverage-balanced descriptive rate but cannot estimate within-stratum prompt-bank sensitivity. The 12-, 96-, and 384-root scopes are reported separately and are never pooled or directly ranked as if they had identical coverage. Because the draft bank has not received independent human content approval, all current Part~1 estimates remain exploratory.

\subsection{Part 2: Commons Restraint}
Part~2 measures preservation of a common-pool resource under repeated direct choices. The repaired simulator implements the incentives stated in the prompt. Each society has five anonymous slots and a 12-day horizon. The common reserve begins at capacity 50. Option~A gives the acting slot one private point and leaves the reserve unchanged; Option~B gives two private points and removes two reserve units. Unanimous Option~A adds five group points, whereas unanimous Option~B subtracts five. After depletion, attrition occurs with probability 0.2 under the common environment seed. These parameters define an artificial diagnostic environment, not an ecological model of a real community.

Each of the 24 matched systems receives eight common environment seeds, and each file is one complete trajectory. Invalid actions have zero state effect and remain nonrestraints. The primary metric is normalized area under the reserve curve:
\[
\mathrm{AURC}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{R_t}{R_{\max}}.
\]
Run-level restraint, area under the population curve, final reserve, population retention, and cumulative private and group payoffs are secondary. The trajectory is the independent unit; agent-days are never inferential replicates. Continuous trajectory metrics receive Student-$t$ 95\% intervals with seven degrees of freedom. Binary reserve nondepletion receives a trajectory-level Wilson interval \citep{wilson1927probable}. No BCa interval, restricted-mean-time-to-depletion analysis, or parameter-sensitivity experiment is part of the executed design.

\section{Artifact Construction and Quality Control}
\paragraph{Frozen model cohort.}
The matched Part~0 and Part~2 panel contains 24 current systems from 9 upstream developers. The registry spans current general-purpose GPT, Claude Haiku/Sonnet/Opus, Gemini, Llama, DeepSeek, Qwen, Nemotron, MiniMax, and GLM routes when the exact authenticated route is callable; technical reports describe selected GPT, GPT-OSS, Llama, and Qwen families, while the route manifests remain authoritative for the exact evaluated identities \citep{openai2023gpt4,openai2025gptoss,touvron2023llama2,yang2024qwen25}. Part~1 targets 81 routes: 75 observed routes run the balanced 96-root schedule, MiniMax~M3 and Qwen3.5~397B run the balanced 12-root schedule, GLM~5.1 runs the full 384-root schedule, and Kimi~K2.5, Kimi~K2.6, and GLM-5.2 are recorded as unavailable. Thus 78 routes are observed and three are unavailable. The broader Part~1 panel never enlarges the matched cross-part sample. Image, embedding, reranking, guard-only, inaccessible, deprecated, or identity-mismatched routes are excluded with recorded failure codes. A display name is never substituted for an unavailable route.

\paragraph{Output contracts and metadata.}
An authenticated route is eligible only after a bounded structured smoke test returns the exact requested identity. Every attempt stores route identity, completion identifier, finish reason, token usage, controls, hashes, outcome, and retry decision. Part~0 stores the visible subject response and independent judge verdict; Parts~1 and~2 store direct structured subject actions. Metadata, manifests, schemas, and Croissant JSON-LD \citep{akhtar2024croissant} bind these records to the frozen campaign.

Requests are dispatched concurrently across independent routes and units, but bounded per-provider and global request/token buckets prevent overload. HTTP 408/429, transport, timeout, and 5xx failures without a retained model response may be retried at most twice with the identical payload and seed. Semantic or formatting failures are retained as \texttt{INVALID}; they are never regenerated. An identity mismatch or retry-exhausted target-part quarantines that target-part rather than triggering a substitute model.

\paragraph{Validation.}
Validation is graph-independent. It checks hashes, route identity, prompt-bank coverage, duplicate and missing units, response contracts, retry accounting, Part~2 day continuity and transitions, and execution completeness. Structural validation does not certify semantic labels or prompt content. Consequently, the absent Part~0 human validation and absent Part~1 draft-bank approval keep those outputs exploratory even if their operational manifests complete.

\paragraph{Harmful-output handling.}
Part 0 evaluates harmful prompts and may produce unsafe completions. The anonymous supplement therefore emphasizes derived tables, validation outputs, and aggregate summaries rather than raw harmful generations. This release choice preserves the evidence used in the paper while limiting unnecessary redistribution of harmful text.

\paragraph{Evidence status.}
Historical pilot artifacts are kept separate from the current campaign and are summarized in Appendix~\ref{app:legacy-evidence}. Current-model manifests are not interpreted until their frozen execution scopes reconcile; operational completeness does not override the exploratory status of Part~0 or Part~1.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
The subject decoding protocol is frozen across routes before outcomes are collected. Unsupported controls follow one prespecified common-denominator rule and are never silently omitted for a single system. The denominator for each task is every scheduled unit; unclear, malformed, and semantic-invalid responses are retained as nonsuccesses. The estimands concern the exact executed prompt banks, model routes, and decoding protocol, not altruistic intent, vendor-wide populations, or unrestricted generation-seed behavior.

Any cross-part analysis is restricted to the exact 24-system matched panel and remains unavailable while Part~0 lacks human validation and the matching Part~1 prompts lack content approval. If those gates are later completed, the paper will report descriptive Spearman coefficients with equal-system, equal-developer, and leave-one-developer-out summaries. It will not report significance tests or treat related systems as an independent model population. Historical systems and the broader Part~1 routes remain separate.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{Partial complementarity without assumed independence.}
Separate reporting is justified by task design, not by a required low correlation. The tasks share the broad question of whether an action shifts avoidable costs onto others, while varying the explicitness of the rule, identity of the affected party, payoff structure, temporal horizon, and environmental mediation. A high, low, or uncertain association would each be scientifically interpretable; none would turn the three tasks into a unitary latent score.

\paragraph{Evidence boundaries.}
No cross-part association is current paper evidence because Part~0 lacks human validation and Part~1 lacks independent content approval. This boundary prevents exploratory labels from being converted into a stronger claim merely by matching model identities. Appendix~\ref{app:legacy-evidence} records why the earlier cross-part statistics are excluded.

\paragraph{Extension to agent systems.}
The same three probes can be applied to tool-using or multi-agent systems by scoring externally visible decisions rather than attributing a stable trait to the underlying language model. Such an extension would require heterogeneous roles, persistent state, communication, tool-mediated actions, independent environments, and system-level failure criteria while preserving the task-specific inferential units used here.

\section{Limitations and Broader Impact}
\label{sec:scope}
Prosocial Readiness Bench evaluates observable outputs in artificial tasks, not intent, moral status, altruism, or real-world trustworthiness. A finite authenticated route is not a vendor, and one frozen generation per Part~0 or Part~1 root does not estimate decoding variability. Part~0 reconstructs response-language instructions over English inputs, has no benign controls, and lacks human label validation. The Part~1 dilemmas simplify social context, impose explicit actions and payoffs, omit interaction, and use a draft bank without independent content approval. Part~2 uses anonymous homogeneous five-agent same-model populations in a 12-step, single-parameterization environment; it omits heterogeneous roles, communication, memory, bargaining, tool use, institutions, and human participants. The executed design contains no parameter-sensitivity study and therefore cannot show robustness beyond this toy ecology.

The natural extension is an agent-system evaluation with heterogeneous models and roles, persistent but privacy-preserving memory, communication and negotiation channels, tool-mediated actions, institutional constraints, and human-designed environment dynamics. Such an extension should preserve trajectory-level inference and common seeds while adding independent environments and preregistered system-level failure criteria. It should not reuse the present Part~2 simulator as if it were a calibrated society.

Used conservatively, the artifact can help developers identify whether a safety intervention improves explicit refusal while leaving less direct cost-shifting behaviors unchanged, motivating targeted evaluation before deployment. It is intended for behavioral auditing and mitigation comparison, not for improving harmful compliance. Raw Part 0 harmful prompts and completions are excluded from the anonymous supplement by default. Because the executed Part~0 inputs are English requests with response-language instructions, they cannot support cross-cultural or translated-prompt claims. Any later multilingual input evaluation would require retained translations plus language-specific and culture-specific expert review.

\section{Conclusion}
Safety Beyond Refusal asks how model evaluations should expand from an explicit harmful request to choices whose costs are less direct, more distributed, and temporally accumulated. Prosocial Readiness Bench operationalizes that progression while keeping refusal, self-directed welfare-preserving choice, and repeated commons preservation separate and only partially complementary. The audit demonstrates why this separation must extend to evidence: invalid labels, mismatched task contracts, and pseudoreplicated agent-days cannot be repaired by attractive plots or narrower intervals. The frozen current execution combines judge--subject separation, broad Part~1 route coverage, root-level inference, and eight common-seed trajectories for each of 24 Part~2 systems. Its limitations are equally concrete: reconstructed rather than translated Part~0 conditions, no Part~0 human validation, an unapproved Part~1 draft bank, and no Part~2 parameter sensitivity. The paper therefore retains defensible legacy Part~1 context and locked result slots without making a current-model ranking or cross-part empirical claim.

\section{Legacy Evidence Note}
\label{app:legacy-evidence}
The April archive contains 56,382 stored rows, but that number is not an inferential sample size: its repeated languages, prompt renderings, and agent-days are clustered. Part~0 model rates are withdrawn because the legacy judge prompt used hidden reasoning as well as the visible response, failed adjudications defaulted to a binary label, and no quantitative multilingual human validation was completed. Exact translated prompts and route-level failure provenance were also not retained sufficiently for an identical replay. Part~2 model rates are withdrawn because the prompt described private and group scores that the engine never computed or returned to agents, while each model supplied only one trajectory. Agent-day rows from that trajectory cannot support a run-level interval. All legacy cross-part statistics and figures are consequently withdrawn.

The April Part~0 heatmaps, Part~2 trajectories and rasters, behavioral fingerprints, PCA projections, and cross-part plots remain in the research archive for provenance but are not paper evidence. Table~\ref{tab:legacy-part1} is the only retained quantitative legacy comparison. It reports only the direct self-choice subset: 48 legacy scenario roots per model, with the two presentations nested within each root. Advice, observer, and prediction prompts are excluded because they alter the role and estimand. The legacy advice, observer, and prediction prompts remain useful as calibration probes, but they do not estimate self-directed cooperation. They may reveal whether a model distinguishes what it would choose, recommend, approve, or expect; such differences are distinct tasks, not a causal framing effect on one construct. If the current Part~0 and Part~1 validity gates are later completed for the exact 24-system overlap, any descriptive Spearman analysis will include equal-system, equal-developer, and leave-one-developer-out summaries. It will not use significance tests or treat related systems as an independent model population.
