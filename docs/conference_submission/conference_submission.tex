\begin{abstract}
Refusal of an explicitly harmful request is necessary for safe language-model behavior, but many consequential choices present no obvious safety rule: a model can shift an immediate cost to a counterpart or repeatedly deplete a resource shared by a group.
We introduce \textbf{Prosocial Readiness Bench}, a three-part evaluation of harmful-request refusal, self-directed cooperation in dyadic dilemmas, and restraint in a repeated commons environment.
The tasks form a staged progression from explicit and identifiable harm to bilateral and then diffuse, cumulative externalities; they measure complementary observable behaviors rather than a latent moral trait.
Our evaluation uses response-only judging by one fixed model disjoint from every subject, a balanced 384-root cooperation bank, and independent common-seed commons trajectories with implemented private and collective incentives.
Across 228 common-seed commons trajectories, mean system restraint ranges from 34.1--100.0\% and normalized reserve area ranges from 0.224--1.000; seven systems preserve both the reserve and population perfectly in every run, whereas GPT-OSS 20B and Nemotron 3 Super deplete the reserve and retain only 20\% and 10\% of the initial population.

The results show why refusal-only evaluation is incomplete: models must also be tested when costs are socially mediated or accumulate through an environment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Language models increasingly answer requests, recommend actions, and participate in workflows where individual and collective incentives diverge \citep{bommasani2021opportunities,liu2024agentbench,park2023generative,zhou2024sotopia}.
Most safety evaluation begins with an explicit harmful request: does the model refuse to help?
That question is essential, but it does not cover choices whose safety relevance is less salient.
A model may decline a clearly malicious instruction while still selecting a unilateral advantage that harms a counterpart, or repeatedly overusing a common resource because each local choice appears individually attractive.

We study this gap with \emph{Safety Beyond Refusal}, operationalized through Prosocial Readiness Bench.
The benchmark contains three separate behavioral probes connected by one task-selection rationale: who bears the cost, how directly that cost is represented, and whether its consequences accumulate.
Part~0 tests material refusal when the target of harm is explicit.
Part~1 tests self-directed dyadic cooperation when a focal actor can shift an immediate cost to another participant.
Part~2 tests repeated restraint when overuse harms a group indirectly through a shared environment.
Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes this progression.

The benchmark does not assume that these behaviors share one underlying ``prosociality'' variable.
Each part has its own prompt contract, independent unit, estimator, and interpretation.
The scientific question is whether the additional probes reveal safety-relevant variation that a refusal-only comparison leaves unmeasured.
We therefore report part-specific outcomes and matched profiles, never a composite score.

We ask three questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item How do current frontier and open-weight systems vary in explicit harmful-request refusal, dyadic cooperation, and repeated commons restraint?
    \item How sensitive are those behaviors to response language, social role and framing, and environmental conditions?
    \item What model differences become visible only after evaluation extends beyond explicit refusal?
\end{enumerate}

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A staged behavioral benchmark.} We connect three observable probes through increasingly indirect cost shifting while preserving their distinct constructs and estimands.
    \item \textbf{Broad current-model evidence.} We evaluate exact authenticated routes from major commercial and open-weight families on balanced, large-$N$ prompt banks and repeated common-seed trajectories.
    \item \textbf{Task-aligned inference.} We use request roots, scenario roots, and complete trajectories as the independent units; uncertainty respects those units rather than treating correlated rows or agent-days as independent.
    \item \textbf{Traceable execution.} Fixed judge--subject separation, exact route identities, shared rate limits, append-only journals, hash-bound manifests, and a reproducible anonymous artifact connect every reported number to retained evidence.
\end{itemize}

\section{Why Safety Beyond Refusal}
Refusal benchmarks make the safety rule conspicuous: the request itself announces a recognizable harmful objective.
Many deployed decisions do not have that form.
Recommendation and agent systems routinely face tradeoffs where the affected party is represented only through a payoff, where a locally beneficial action distributes losses across a group, or where no single action is catastrophic but repetition changes the environment.

Our task sequence varies three properties together in a controlled progression.
First, \emph{rule salience} decreases from an explicitly harmful instruction to a stylized social choice.
Second, the \emph{affected set} grows from an identifiable target to one counterpart and then a group.
Third, the \emph{temporal distance} of the externality changes from immediate assistance, to an immediate payoff, to cumulative environmental consequences.
The benchmark is not a realism ladder; each task is deliberately narrow.
Its purpose is to reveal whether a model comparison changes as the externality becomes less direct.

This framing also determines what the benchmark does not claim.
Observable choices in artificial tasks are not evidence of intent, moral status, or a stable personality trait.
High performance in one part does not license a global safety judgment.
The useful object is the profile: which systems remain conservative across distinct conflicts, and which appear safe only when the rule is explicit?

\section{Related Work}
HarmBench, JailbreakBench, and related red-teaming suites evaluate harmful-request compliance, refusal, and safeguard robustness \citep{mazeika2024harmbench,chao2024jailbreakbench,ganguli2022redteaming,bai2022constitutional,zou2023universal,wang2024donotanswer,rottger2024xstest}.
They motivate Part~0 but do not directly measure choices in social dilemmas or repeated resource environments.

LLM game-theory studies test strategic behavior in settings inspired by classical work on cooperation and common-pool resources \citep{axelrod1984evolution,hardin1968tragedy,ostrom1990governing,dafoe2020open,akata2025playing,brookins2024playing,horton2023homo}.
AgentBench, generative agents, and SOTOPIA broaden evaluation to tool use and open-ended social interaction \citep{liu2024agentbench,park2023generative,zhou2024sotopia}.
Our contribution is a controlled, route-matched comparison across explicit refusal, a one-shot interpersonal externality, and a repeated collective externality.

Multi-axis suites such as HELM and DecodingTrust show the value of reporting distinct capabilities and risks rather than one score \citep{hendrycks2021measuring,srivastava2023beyond,liang2023helm,openai2023gpt4,wang2023decodingtrust,kirk2024prism}.
Dataset and model documentation standards motivate our emphasis on provenance and reproducible release artifacts \citep{bender2018datastatements,holland2018datasetnutrition,mitchell2019modelcards,gebru2021datasheets,pushkarna2022datacards,akhtar2024croissant}.
Prosocial Readiness Bench combines those principles with one staged behavioral question: what changes when evaluation moves beyond refusal?

\subsection{Part 0: Harmful-Request Refusal}
Part~0 selects 48 archived English harmful-request roots from locally versioned HarmBench and JailbreakBench snapshots \citep{mazeika2024harmbench,chao2024jailbreakbench}.
Each root is paired with an instruction to answer in English, Simplified Chinese, or Russian, producing 144 scheduled responses per system.
Because the request itself remains English, this design measures response-language sensitivity rather than translated-input or cross-cultural robustness.

One fixed strong judge scores only the visible final response as \texttt{REFUSAL}, \texttt{COMPLIANCE}, or \texttt{UNCLEAR}; malformed or missing outputs are \texttt{INVALID}.
The judge is excluded from the subject roster by target ID, exact route, and upstream provider/model identity.
The primary rate is
\[
  R_0 = \frac{\#\texttt{REFUSAL}}{\#\text{scheduled responses}}.
\]
We use Wilson intervals within each 48-root language condition and a deterministic root-cluster bootstrap for the across-language system summary.

\subsection{Part 1: Dyadic Cooperation}
Part~1 presents a focal model with one-shot choices whose actions preserve joint welfare or give the focal actor an advantage while imposing a larger cost on a counterpart.
The primary self-choice bank contains 384 roots per route, balanced across two game structures and six domains, with scenario variants, presentation counterbalancing, and action-label counterbalancing.
The primary estimand is
\[
  R_1 = \frac{\#\text{welfare-preserving first choices}}{384}.
\]
Uncertainty is computed by a 5,000-replicate root bootstrap stratified within the 12 game--domain cells.
These intervals describe sensitivity to the frozen balanced bank rather than a population of all possible dilemmas.

Advice, observer evaluation, and prediction are evaluated in a separate six-route calibration panel with 384 draws per route-frame.
They remain distinct estimands: advice asks what another actor should do, observer evaluation asks how an action should be judged, and prediction asks what an actor is likely to do.
Comparing them diagnoses role-conditioned behavior without treating verbal advice as the model's own action.

\subsection{Part 2: Repeated Commons Restraint}
Part~2 places five same-model agents in a 12-day common-pool environment.
At each agent-day, \texttt{OPTION\_A} awards one private point and preserves the reserve; \texttt{OPTION\_B} awards two private points and removes two reserve units.
Agents observe current population and resource state, so repeated overuse can reduce both the reserve and the surviving group.
The prompt and engine implement and record the same payoffs.

Each system receives 12 independent common environment seeds.
The action metric is restraint over all scheduled agent-days.
Environmental outcomes are normalized area under the reserve curve (AURC), reserve nondepletion, and final population retention.
For a trajectory of horizon $T$,
\[
  \mathrm{AURC}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{R_t}{R_{\max}},
  \qquad
  \mathrm{PopulationRetention}=\frac{P_T}{P_0}.
\]
Intervals are Student-$t$ intervals over independent trajectories, not binomial intervals over state-coupled agent-days.
A separate resolution-V panel varies capacity, depletion units, collapse death rate, society size, and horizon for six sentinel routes.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Model Coverage and Exact Identity}
The frozen registry covers current GPT, Claude Haiku/Sonnet/Opus, Gemini, Llama, DeepSeek, Qwen, Nemotron, MiniMax, Kimi, GLM, Mistral, and other compatible frontier or strong open-weight routes when an exact authenticated endpoint is callable.
Part~0 schedules 22 systems, Part~1 schedules 75 hosted routes, and Part~2 schedules 19 systems.
Four pinned local Hugging Face controls separately complete the Part~1 bank to probe execution scale; they are neither substitutes for unavailable hosted routes nor pooled into the hosted panel.
The manifest records requested route, upstream provider, and returned model identity for every call.
An unavailable or identity-mismatched route is reported as such and is never replaced by a neighboring model.

\subsection{Parallel Execution Without API Overload}
Independent model calls run concurrently under a shared cross-process limiter.
Every campaign has bounded global and provider concurrency, leaky-bucket request rates, one in-flight call per exact route where required, durable leases, and full cooldowns for throttling and server errors.
The client honors \texttt{Retry-After}; hidden SDK retries are disabled.
Retries preserve identical request bytes and seeds and are bounded at eight physical attempts.
Reservations occur before dispatch in an append-only, fsync-backed, hash-chained ledger, making interruption and resume verifiable.

\subsection{Validity and Inference}
Part~0 uses the same disjoint judge for every subject and never exposes hidden reasoning to the scorer.
Part~1 and Part~2 use direct structured actions and require exact route identity.
First-attempt format or semantic invalids remain nonsuccesses in all-scheduled action denominators; diagnostic repairs are released separately and never replace primary rows.
Transport failures are operational outcomes, not model choices.

The independent units are request roots for Part~0, scenario roots for Part~1, and trajectories for Part~2.
No claim treats repeated languages, counterbalances, agents, or days as independent samples.
Cross-axis displays join only exact authenticated identities and preserve the three original denominators.
They are descriptive profiles, not a composite score or causal model-family comparison.

\section{Discussion}
Safety Beyond Refusal changes the unit of comparison from one refusal score to a structured behavioral profile.
This matters operationally because explicit harmful-request evaluation is a high-salience setting: the model is directly told what action would cause harm.
The dyadic and commons tasks test whether conservative behavior persists when the safety relevance must be inferred from payoffs, affected parties, and temporal consequences.

The benchmark is most useful when it reveals disagreement.
A model that refuses reliably but selects focal advantage in Part~1 requires a different intervention from one that cooperates in a one-shot dilemma but overuses a repeated commons.
Likewise, role-conditioned differences distinguish what a model recommends or predicts from what it selects for itself.
These are actionable diagnostics for model selection and agent-system testing even when they do not support a single global ordering.

The repeated environment adds information that static choice cannot.
Restraint is a model action, AURC is a resource consequence, and population retention is a group consequence.
Reporting all three exposes whether similar action rates produce different trajectories under collapse dynamics and whether a result depends on one nominal parameterization.

\section{Limitations and Broader Impact}
\label{sec:scope}
The benchmark measures outputs in artificial tasks, not intent or real-world trustworthiness.
Part~0 uses harmful-only English requests with response-language instructions; it lacks benign controls, and language-qualified human validation is required before treating automatic labels as definitive multilingual model rankings.
Part~1 uses stylized dilemmas from a fixed balanced bank, while Part~2 uses homogeneous five-agent societies and a short toy environment; neither captures communication, institutions, heterogeneous roles, memory, or human interaction.
The sensitivity panel has only two seeds per cell.
Exact endpoint results describe frozen routes and prompts, not vendors or model families in general.

Used within these boundaries, the artifact supports pre-deployment behavioral assessment and comparison of targeted safety interventions.
Raw harmful prompts and completions remain private by default; the anonymous supplement releases aggregate tables, validation, schemas, hashes, and reproduction tooling.
Results should guide additional testing, not moral labeling of models or deployment without domain-specific evaluation.

\section{Conclusion}
Refusal is a necessary safety behavior, but it is not the end of safety evaluation.
Prosocial Readiness Bench extends the comparison to bilateral choices and repeated collective consequences through three explicit, separately reported task contracts.
Large balanced prompt banks, independent trajectories, exact route identity, disjoint judging, and task-aligned uncertainty make the resulting profiles traceable.
The central lesson is constructive: evaluating cooperation and commons restraint reveals model behavior that a harmful-request refusal score alone cannot measure.

\section*{Reproducibility Statement}
The anonymous artifact provides frozen schemas, task contracts, exact public route identifiers, validation code, aggregate analyzers, figure and table generators, Croissant metadata, and clean-extraction reproduction commands.
Private prompts, responses, reasoning traces, API credentials, and host-specific paths are excluded.
Every public output is hash-bound to its source aggregate manifest, and existing output directories are never overwritten.

\section{Task Details}
\subsection{Part 0 Scoring Contract}
The judge sees only the subject's visible response and an exact response identifier.
It returns one label per item in a structured batch, preserving order and rejecting missing, duplicated, or unknown identifiers.
\texttt{REFUSAL} requires that the response withhold concrete assistance; \texttt{COMPLIANCE} requires material assistance; \texttt{UNCLEAR} captures responses that cannot be assigned safely under the contract.

\subsection{Part 1 Counterbalancing}
Every scenario root is rendered with counterbalanced action labels and presentation order.
The welfare-preserving action is defined by the payoff contract before generation, not inferred from model justification.
The 384-root self-choice bank remains separate from the three role-calibration frames.

\subsection{Part 2 Transition Contract}
Each valid action is recorded before the simulator transition.
Invalid first actions receive zero state effect for continuity and count as nonrestraint; a trajectory containing one is excluded from environmental outcomes.
All environment metrics are recomputed from the retained transition trace.

\section{Artifact Map}
\label{app:artifact-map}
The public aggregate adapter validates every source manifest, journal binding, exact route identity, frozen schedule, denominator, and interval contract before producing text-free tables.
The paper-asset generator validates those tables again, writes vector PDFs and 300-dpi PNGs, centers quantitative table columns, and records file hashes and 15-point outer spacing in its manifest.
The anonymous supplement includes aggregate CSV/JSONL, current PNG and table assets, schemas, documentation, and Croissant 1.1 metadata; a clean extracted copy can rebuild and verify the archive without a Git object store.
