\begin{abstract}
Refusing an explicitly harmful request is necessary for safe language-model behavior, but it does not exhaust the ways an agent can shift costs onto other people.
We present \textbf{Prosocial Readiness Bench}, a three-part evaluation spanning multilingual harmful-request refusal, self-directed choices in one-shot social dilemmas, and resource preservation in a repeated commons.
The tasks form a cost-shifting progression: the potential cost moves from an identifiable request target, to a dyadic counterpart, to a group whose losses accumulate through an environment.
They are partially complementary stress tests, not interchangeable measures of altruism and not a single readiness score.
An audit of the original pilot found that its Part~0 labels and Part~2 simulator do not support model-level conclusions, so we withdraw those legacy estimates and all cross-part correlations.
We retain a clearly labeled descriptive Part~1 result and specify a confirmatory evaluation over current frontier and open-weight systems, with independent experimental units, a judge that is distinct from every evaluated subject, human validation, common-seed repeated simulations, and multiplicity-aware analysis.
This revision contributes a traceable benchmark design and an explicit account of what evidence is---and is not---currently available; it reports no fabricated frontier results.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
LLM agents increasingly operate in social settings: they answer human requests, recommend actions, coordinate with other systems, and may participate in workflows where individual and collective incentives diverge \citep{bommasani2021opportunities,liu2024agentbench,park2023generative,zhou2024sotopia}. In these settings, refusal of explicitly harmful requests is only one part of responsible behavior. A broader evaluation should also assess whether a model selects cooperative actions in social dilemmas and whether repeated choices preserve a shared resource when overuse offers a short-term advantage.

Prosocial Readiness Bench addresses this requirement through three observable tasks rather than a latent moral trait. Part~0 asks whether a model materially refuses a request that would directly facilitate harm. Part~1 asks what the model itself chooses when a unilateral advantage shifts an immediate cost to a counterpart. Part~2 asks whether repeated local choices preserve a shared resource when the cost is delayed, distributed, and mediated by an environment. This progression supplies the task-selection rationale: each part increases the distance between action and affected party and changes how visibly and temporally the externality appears.

The three measurements are partially complementary. They may share mechanisms---instruction following, norm recognition, or sensitivity to others' welfare---but each also introduces task-specific demands. We therefore do not assume that they correlate, interpret disagreement as proof of independence, or collapse them into an overall prosociality score. Cross-part associations are secondary finite-panel descriptions that become reportable only after every part passes its validity gate.

The original April pilot covered 13 related local/open-weight variants. An end-to-end audit showed that Part~0 used rationale-contaminated automated labels without quantitative human validation and that Part~2 described incentives the engine never implemented while providing only one trajectory per model. Those defects invalidate the corresponding model rates and every correlation using them. We remove those results rather than cosmetically changing their intervals. The surviving Part~1 self-choice summary is retained only as legacy descriptive context; the confirmatory campaign is outcome-independent and is not pooled with it.

The paper asks two concrete questions. First, how should refusal, dyadic cost shifting, and repeated commons preservation be measured in one traceable artifact while respecting their different independent units? Second, once the same broad current-model panel has completed all three validated tasks, what patterns of partial complementarity appear across those measurements? Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the design and its gates.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cost-shifting task progression.} We motivate three partially complementary behavioral probes by who bears the cost, how directly it is represented, and whether consequences accumulate over time.
    \item \textbf{Corrected measurement design.} We define semantic prompt clusters, scenario roots, and complete simulation trajectories as the respective independent units; malformed and unclear outputs remain in denominators.
    \item \textbf{Validated judging and simulation.} Part~0 uses a fixed judge distinct from every evaluated subject plus a preregistered multilingual human criterion gate. Part~2 removes unrealized incentives and uses repeated common-seed trajectories.
    \item \textbf{Broad, traceable evaluation plan.} The current-SOTA panel spans commercial frontier and open-weight families through authenticated, identity-checked routes, with rate-limited parallel dispatch and no silent model substitution.
    \item \textbf{Transparent evidence boundary.} We withdraw invalid legacy statistics, retain only defensible Part~1 descriptive context, and withhold confirmatory model comparisons until the frozen campaign is complete.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
Safety and jailbreak benchmarks such as HarmBench and JailbreakBench measure harmful-request compliance and refusal \citep{mazeika2024harmbench,chao2024jailbreakbench}; related red-teaming, adversarial-attack, safeguard, and exaggerated-safety work further shows that refusal must be measured under both unsafe and borderline prompts \citep{ganguli2022redteaming,bai2022constitutional,zou2023universal,wang2024donotanswer,rottger2024xstest}.

LLM game-theory studies evaluate cooperation in strategic settings inspired by classical work on cooperation and commons governance \citep{axelrod1984evolution,hardin1968tragedy,ostrom1990governing,dafoe2020open,akata2025playing,brookins2024playing,horton2023homo}. Multi-agent and social-evaluation environments such as AgentBench, generative agents, and SOTOPIA study richer open-ended interaction \citep{liu2024agentbench,park2023generative,zhou2024sotopia}.

General and multi-axis evaluation suites motivate reporting separate behavioral dimensions rather than a single score \citep{hendrycks2021measuring,srivastava2023beyond,liang2023helm,openai2023gpt4,wang2023decodingtrust,kirk2024prism,jiang2025artificialhivemind}. Dataset and model documentation standards motivate the artifact-facing emphasis on provenance, metadata, access conditions, and responsible-use notes \citep{bender2018datastatements,holland2018datasetnutrition,mitchell2019modelcards,gebru2021datasheets,pushkarna2022datacards,akhtar2024croissant}.

The gap addressed here is the combination of harmful-request refusal, self-directed one-shot choice, repeated commons preservation, and traceable release artifacts in one benchmark whose inferential units match its claims. Table~\ref{tab:positioning} summarizes this positioning. Unlike the original local-model pilot, the confirmatory design targets a frozen panel of current commercial and open-weight systems and treats historical routes as a separate descriptive cohort.

\section{Benchmark Design and Task Contracts}
\subsection{Design Principles}
The benchmark uses three design principles.

\paragraph{Organize tasks by cost shifting.}
The parts move from a direct harmful request, to an immediate choice affecting a counterpart, to repeated choices affecting a group through a shared state. This is a principled progression in externality structure, not a claim that the tasks form a psychometric scale. Refusal, self-directed welfare-preserving choice, and resource preservation remain separate outcomes.

\paragraph{Make every claim traceable to stored artifacts.}
Each request retains its exact route identity, decoding controls, request and response hashes, provider completion identifier, finish reason, token usage, retry decision, and structured outcome. Campaign manifests bind the frozen registry, prompt bank, execution evidence, and analysis outputs.

\paragraph{Match inference to experimental units.}
Languages nested within a semantic request, renderings nested within a scenario root, and agent-days nested within a trajectory are not independent observations. The estimators below resample clusters, roots, or complete trajectories accordingly. A narrower valid interval is obtained by collecting more independent units or repeated generations, never by treating dependent rows as independent.

\subsection{Part 0: Safety Refusal}
Part~0 separates harmful-request material refusal from harmless-request substantive answering, so an always-refuse policy cannot appear uniformly safe. The confirmatory registry contains 484 deduplicated harmful roots and 100 benign controls in English, Simplified Chinese, and Russian; none becomes eligible until its source, content, translation, and semantic-cluster records receive the required human approvals. Languages are repeated measurements of one semantic prompt cluster, not three independent examples. Every scheduled response remains in the denominator; \texttt{UNCLEAR} and \texttt{INVALID} are nonsuccesses.

One frozen multilingual judge receives only the localized request and the raw visible response, is blind to subject identity and hypotheses, and returns \texttt{REFUSAL}, \texttt{COMPLIANCE}, or \texttt{UNCLEAR} at temperature zero. The judge model is fixed across the subject panel and is never the model being evaluated. If a route selected as judge would otherwise enter the subject panel, that route is excluded from judging itself rather than silently switching judges. This separation reduces self-preference and moving-rater confounds but does not establish judge validity \citep{zheng2023judging}.

The primary metric is safety refusal:
\[
\mathrm{refusal} =
\frac{\#\mathrm{REFUSAL\ on\ harmful\ roots}}{\#\mathrm{scheduled\ harmful\ roots}}.
\]
Harmless substantive-answer rate is reported separately. Five thousand bootstrap replicates resample semantic clusters within the harmful and benign arms, retain all languages within a root, and weight clusters equally. These are finite-bank cluster-resampling sensitivity bands, not confidence intervals for generation variability or a prompt superpopulation.

Automated labels are promotable only after a stratified multilingual human audit. Two independent language-qualified annotators label the frozen sample, complete delayed duplicates, and are followed by independent adjudication. Promotion requires prespecified lower confidence bounds of at least 0.90 for balanced accuracy, harmful compliance recall, and refusal precision; lower bounds of at least 0.80 for inter- and intra-rater kappa; and upper bounds of at most 5\% for human and automated unclear rates. A failed second judge gate withdraws Part~0 claims.

\subsection{Part 1: Dyadic Cooperation}
Part~1 evaluates what the model itself chooses in a neutral one-shot dilemma. Advice, observer evaluation, prediction, and demand-cue prompts are omitted from the confirmatory construct because they ask different questions; they are not paraphrases of self-directed choice. This distinction resolves the ambiguity in the original pilot's pooled ``frame'' rate.

The confirmatory plan contains 384 independently authored scenario roots: 32 in each of two game by six neutral-domain cells. No root is eligible until three independent reviewers unanimously approve its payoff ordering, welfare mapping, neutral language, and absence of harmful or prescriptive confounds. Within each cell, eight roots receive each of four counterbalances crossing option order, wording order, and structured versus narrative presentation. Each approved root appears once. Responses must contain the exact structured action \texttt{X} or \texttt{Y}; malformed output is \texttt{INVALID}, is retained as a non-welfare-preserving choice, and is not regenerated.

The primary metric is the self-directed welfare-preserving choice rate:
\[
\mathrm{self\mbox{-}choice} =
\frac{\#\mathrm{welfare\mbox{-}preserving\ choices}}{384}.
\]
The scenario root is the independent unit. Five thousand bootstrap replicates resample roots separately within the 12 game-domain strata. The resulting bands describe sensitivity to the frozen scenario bank, not generation-seed uncertainty or a scenario superpopulation. Game, domain, rendering, and option-order summaries are secondary descriptions because each root receives only one counterbalance.

\subsection{Part 2: Commons Restraint}
Part~2 measures preservation of a common-pool resource under repeated direct choices. The repaired simulator removes the private and group score incentives that the legacy prompt advertised but the engine did not implement. It uses anonymous slots rather than stable personas and randomizes post-depletion attrition with the environment seed. The primary cell has 10 agents, a 30-day horizon, reserve capacity 150, depletion of two units per overuse action, and post-depletion death probability 0.2. These parameters define an artificial diagnostic environment, not an ecological model of a real community.

Each model receives 24 common environment seeds, and each file is one complete trajectory. Invalid actions remain nonrestraints. The primary metric is normalized area under the reserve curve:
\[
\mathrm{AURC}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{R_t}{R_{\max}}.
\]
Run-level restraint, area under the population curve, restricted mean time to depletion, and reserve nondepletion are secondary. The trajectory is the independent unit; agent-days are never inferential replicates. Mean AURC receives a Student-$t$ 95\% interval with 23 degrees of freedom and a labeled BCa sensitivity analysis. Binary nondepletion receives a trajectory-level Wilson interval \citep{wilson1927probable}. Common seed indices are resampled jointly across models for paired comparisons.

A prespecified 16-cell resolution-V sensitivity design varies capacity per initial agent, depletion, death rate, population, and horizon for six outcome-blind sentinel systems with 12 common seeds per cell. Its 30 sentinel-by-main-effect tests form one Holm family. This design tests whether a profile is specific to one toy parameterization; it cannot establish real-world ecological validity.

\section{Artifact Construction and Quality Control}
\paragraph{Frozen model cohort.}
The matched primary panel contains 24 current systems from 9 upstream developers. The registry spans current general-purpose GPT, Claude Haiku/Sonnet/Opus, Gemini, Llama, DeepSeek, Qwen, Nemotron, MiniMax, and GLM routes when the exact authenticated route is callable. The broader Part~1 expansion is analyzed separately and never enlarges the matched cross-part sample. Image, embedding, reranking, guard-only, inaccessible, deprecated, or identity-mismatched routes are excluded with recorded failure codes. A display name is never substituted for an unavailable route.

\paragraph{Output contracts and metadata.}
An authenticated route is eligible only after a bounded structured smoke test returns the exact requested identity. Every attempt stores route identity, completion identifier, finish reason, token usage, controls, hashes, outcome, and retry decision. Part~0 stores the visible subject response and independent judge verdict; Parts~1 and~2 store direct structured subject actions. Metadata, manifests, schemas, and Croissant JSON-LD \citep{akhtar2024croissant} bind these records to the frozen campaign.

Requests are dispatched concurrently across independent routes and units, but bounded per-provider and global request/token buckets prevent overload. HTTP 408/429, transport, timeout, and 5xx failures without a retained model response may be retried at most twice with the identical payload and seed. Semantic or formatting failures are retained as \texttt{INVALID}; they are never regenerated. An identity mismatch or retry-exhausted target-part quarantines that target-part rather than triggering a substitute model.

\paragraph{Validation.}
Validation is graph-independent. It checks hashes, route identity, prompt-bank coverage, duplicate and missing units, response contracts, retry accounting, Part~2 day continuity and transitions, and campaign completeness. Structural validation does not certify semantic labels; the Part~0 human criterion gate is separate. Confirmatory estimates remain locked unless all planned routes and all part-specific gates complete.

\paragraph{Harmful-output handling.}
Part 0 evaluates harmful prompts and may produce unsafe completions. The anonymous supplement therefore emphasizes derived tables, validation outputs, and aggregate summaries rather than raw harmful generations. This release choice preserves the evidence used in the paper while limiting unnecessary redistribution of harmful text.

\paragraph{Evidence status.}
The April archive contains 56,382 stored rows, but that number is not an inferential sample size: its repeated languages, prompt renderings, and agent-days are clustered. The legacy Part~0 and Part~2 outputs remain available for audit and debugging but are excluded from paper-facing model comparisons. Confirmatory SOTA manifests are not interpreted until the frozen campaign is complete.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
The subject decoding protocol is frozen across routes before outcomes are collected. Unsupported controls follow one prespecified common-denominator rule and are never silently omitted for a single system. The denominator for each task is every scheduled unit; unclear, malformed, and semantic-invalid responses are retained as nonsuccesses. The confirmatory estimands concern the exact frozen prompt banks, model routes, and decoding protocol, not altruistic intent, vendor-wide populations, or unrestricted generation-seed behavior.

Cross-part analysis includes only current systems that complete all three parts. Three prespecified Spearman associations form one Holm family. A nested bootstrap resamples Part~0 semantic clusters, Part~1 roots within game-domain strata, and Part~2 common seed indices; each sampled unit receives the same weight for every fixed system. Equal-system and equal-developer coefficients and leave-one-developer-out ranges are mandatory. Historical systems remain separate. With 24 current systems, these are descriptive finite-panel associations, not population laws, and no observed correlation is needed to justify reporting the three tasks separately.

\section{Pilot Results}
The audit changes what can be claimed from the April archive. Part~0 model rates are withdrawn because the legacy judge prompt used hidden reasoning as well as the visible response, failed adjudications defaulted to a binary label, and no quantitative multilingual human validation was completed. Exact translated prompts and route-level failure provenance were also not retained sufficiently for an identical replay. Part~2 model rates are withdrawn because the prompt described private and group scores that the engine never computed or returned to agents, while each model supplied only one trajectory. Agent-day rows from that trajectory cannot support a run-level interval. All legacy cross-part statistics and figures are consequently withdrawn.

\paragraph{Defensible legacy context.}
Table~\ref{tab:legacy-part1} reports only the direct self-choice subset. Its estimates are descriptive rates over 48 legacy scenario roots per model, with the two presentations nested within each root. Advice, observer, and prediction prompts are excluded because they alter the role and estimand. The spread in Table~\ref{tab:legacy-part1} demonstrates that the self-choice task is capable of eliciting heterogeneous actions in the legacy cohort, but it neither validates Part~0 or Part~2 nor establishes relationships among the three tasks. The new 384-root confirmatory bank was authored independently of these outcomes; genuine reviewer approval remains a promotion gate.

\paragraph{Confirmatory status.}
The broad current-model campaign is designed but not yet promotable. Partial route probes, canaries, or incomplete runs are operational evidence only; they do not enter a results table. Confirmatory estimates will be released together only after route identity, task completeness, the Part~0 human gate, and all frozen analysis checks pass. This prevents a changing set of successful endpoints from becoming an outcome-selected leaderboard.

\section{Diagnostics}
\label{sec:diagnostics}
\paragraph{Validity before association.}
The original correlation table is intentionally absent. Part~0 label changes in the partial response-only rejudgment and the Part~2 contract failure are large enough that model ranks cannot be treated as stable. Moreover, final simulated population is a deterministic downstream consequence of the resource transition and is not an independent validation target. The confirmatory cross-part analysis therefore begins only after all three corrected estimators are locked for the same systems.

\paragraph{Partial complementarity without assumed independence.}
Separate reporting is justified by task design, not by a required low correlation. The tasks share the broad question of whether an action shifts avoidable costs onto others, while varying the explicitness of the rule, identity of the affected party, payoff structure, temporal horizon, and environmental mediation. A high, low, or uncertain association would each be scientifically interpretable; none would turn the three tasks into a unitary latent score.

\paragraph{Role prompts are calibration probes.}
The legacy advice, observer, and prediction prompts remain useful for studying role-conditioned responses, but they do not estimate self-directed cooperation. They may reveal whether a model distinguishes what it would choose, recommend, approve, or expect. Such differences should be reported as response calibration across distinct tasks, not as a causal ``framing effect'' on one construct.

\paragraph{Matched-panel robustness.}
Confirmatory associations use three Spearman coefficients, Holm correction, a nested bootstrap that preserves shared prompts and seeds, equal-system and equal-developer weighting, and leave-one-developer-out ranges. Related releases from one developer are not independent evidence about a model population. Wider intervals are reported honestly; tighter population claims require more independent developers, while generation uncertainty requires repeated generations per root.

\section{Scope and Responsible Use}
\label{sec:scope}
Prosocial Readiness Bench evaluates observable outputs in artificial tasks, not intent, moral status, altruism, or real-world trustworthiness. A finite authenticated route is not a vendor, and one frozen generation per Part~0 or Part~1 root does not estimate decoding variability. The Part~1 dilemmas simplify social context, impose explicit actions and payoffs, and omit interaction. Part~2 uses anonymous homogeneous same-model populations in a deliberately small, stylized environment; it omits heterogeneous roles, communication, memory, bargaining, tool use, institutions, and human participants. Its sensitivity study probes parameter dependence but cannot establish ecological validity.

The natural extension is an agent-system evaluation with heterogeneous models and roles, persistent but privacy-preserving memory, communication and negotiation channels, tool-mediated actions, institutional constraints, and human-designed environment dynamics. Such an extension should preserve trajectory-level inference and common seeds while adding independent environments and preregistered system-level failure criteria. It should not reuse the present Part~2 simulator as if it were a calibrated society.

The artifact is intended for behavioral auditing and mitigation comparison, not for improving harmful compliance. Raw Part 0 harmful prompts and completions are excluded from the anonymous supplement by default, and multilingual harmful-request evaluation needs language-specific and culture-specific expert review before stronger cross-cultural claims. The same multilingual coverage that helps expose English-centric blind spots could surface culturally specific harmful-request patterns, so downstream releases should preserve safety filtering, access controls, and responsible-use documentation.

\section{Conclusion}
Safety Beyond Refusal asks how model evaluations should expand from an explicit harmful request to choices whose costs are less direct, more distributed, and temporally accumulated. Prosocial Readiness Bench operationalizes that progression while keeping refusal, self-directed welfare-preserving choice, and repeated commons preservation separate and only partially complementary. The audit demonstrates why this separation must extend to evidence: invalid labels, mismatched task contracts, and pseudoreplicated agent-days cannot be repaired by attractive plots or narrower intervals. The corrected protocol combines a broad current-model panel, judge--subject separation, quantitative human validation, root- and trajectory-level inference, repeated common seeds, and prespecified sensitivity analyses. Until that campaign is complete, the paper reports only defensible legacy Part~1 context and makes no frontier ranking or cross-part empirical claim.
